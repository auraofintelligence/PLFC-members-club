# Fishing guide source and content decisions

Sources were checked on 20 November 2026. This guide concerns recreational fishing around Point Lookout and Moreton Bay. It is a club planning resource, not a complete legal determination or a live safety assessment.

## Rules and seasonal checks

- Queensland Department of Primary Industries tidal closed seasons: https://www.dpi.qld.gov.au/services/fisheries/closures/tidal-seasons
- Current recreational size and possession table: https://www.qld.gov.au/recreation/activities/boating-fishing/rec-fishing/rules/limits-tidal
- Official identification hub: https://www.qld.gov.au/recreation/activities/boating-fishing/rec-fishing/fish-species-guide/fish-species-id

The closed-seasons page was updated on 25 September 2026. It lists an annual east-coast snapper and pearl perch closure from 15 July to 15 August, and southern Spanish mackerel closures from 1 to 21 February and 1 to 21 March. Southern means south of 22 degrees south, which includes Point Lookout. Northern Spanish mackerel dates have not been applied to this location.

The trip helper tests the inclusive dates for 2026 and 2027 only. It never returns an open, legal or safe decision. Outside those years, or with an invalid date, it asks for a fresh official check. A date outside either selected pattern does not rule out other restrictions.

The guide deliberately links current limit tables instead of reproducing numerical species limits. Species identification, combined possession limits, boat limits, whole-fish requirements, gear, zoning and other closures may change or depend on circumstances.

## Marine park rules

- Maps and official apps: https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/moreton-bay/zoning/app-and-maps
- Activity provisions and designated areas: https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/moreton-bay/zoning/provisions-tables

The provisions table confirms that fishing and collecting are prohibited in marine national park green zones. Other zones have conditions and designated areas can impose additional restrictions. The app does not determine the user's zone or authorise a fishing location.

Use the current MSQ lifejacket page, updated 16 April 2026. An older general Queensland boating safety page still describes older requirements, so it was not used to summarise the law.

The fisheries reporting page confirms Fishwatch 1800 017 116 operates 24 hours a day, seven days a week. It accepts suspected illegal fishing, abandoned gear and habitat damage reports, including anonymous online reports. Emergency 000 is separately confirmed by Queensland Police.

- Sand whiting background: https://australian.museum/learn/animals/fishes/sand-whiting-sillago-ciliata-cuvier-1829/

- Southern Queensland monitoring context: https://www.dpi.qld.gov.au/services/fisheries/monitor/rec/keen-angler

The eight fish are sand whiting, yellowfin bream, dusky flathead, tailor, snapper, Spanish mackerel, swallowtail dart and mulloway. Bait, technique and broad timing suggestions are explicitly labelled general club tips. They are not attributed to Fisheries Queensland as approved techniques and are not forecasts of local availability.

Each fish card uses a real photograph. [Photo credits and licences](../public/fish-photos.js) records the scientific name, photographer, exact source file, licence, attribution link and changes for each image. The local WebP copies carry the corresponding source licences. The images show individual fish photographed in several locations. For example, the yellowfin bream is from the Manning River and the snapper was photographed at Melbourne Aquarium. For identification and applicable limits, use the linked Fisheries Queensland guide.

The setup cards give an example for each target and explain how to adjust it. [The larger species examples](../public/heavy-fish-rigs.js) link to the original angler, government and tackle references beside each setup. Some combine compatible choices from two sources, such as the NSW Coffs Coast snapper leader range and the trace length illustrated by Tackle Tactics. A manufacturer's treble size applies to that particular diving minnow. Gear references from other states inform practical technique. Queensland sources govern local rules.

The contextual helper uses only the selected date, method and target. It does not fetch a user's position, account data or live weather, and does not make an AI legal judgement. Review this content again before 2028 and whenever relevant rules change.
